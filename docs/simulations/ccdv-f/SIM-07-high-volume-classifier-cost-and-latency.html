CCDV-F SIM-07 — Cutting Cost and Latency on a High-Volume Classifier

How to use this: read the scene, then work through the decisions on paper, or paste the scene and the decisions list (not the answer key) into Claude and ask it to run the simulation one decision at a time with no hints. Open the coach key only when you've finished.

Primary domains: 5 Model Selection and Optimization, 6 Prompt and Context Engineering · Decisions: 10 · Time: 20 min

Scene (original, written from the official objectives)

An online marketplace uses Claude to sort every new listing into one of 40 categories and flag prohibited items. Sellers see the result a few seconds after they post, and hundreds of thousands of listings arrive each day. The classifier runs on the most capable model at a high effort level, with a long instruction block of category definitions and 30 examples, and each answer explains its reasoning in a paragraph. Accuracy is good, but the bill doubled this quarter and sellers complain about the wait. You're asked to cut both without losing accuracy.

The decisions (answer these before you open the key)

  1. What to do before changing anything
  2. The first lever to try on the current model
  3. Most listings are easy; a few (possible weapons, medicines) are hard calls
  4. Output tokens are most of the bill, and nobody reads the reasoning paragraph
  5. The long instruction block is identical on every call and paid for at full price
  6. After a clean-up trims the instructions to a few hundred tokens, both cache fields in usage read 0, and no error appears
  7. The 30 examples are all easy categories, and edge cases are still misclassified
  8. Category definitions are pasted after the listing text, mixed in with it
  9. A taxonomy change means re-labeling millions of existing listings by next Monday
  10. After the switch, a few answers use a different capitalization of a category, or come back with stop_reason: "refusal"

8Category definitions are pasted after the listing text, mixed in with itPut the stable definitions in the system prompt (which also keeps the cached prefix stable) and wrap the listing in <listing> tags in the user turnMix everything into one paragraph; put the definitions inside the listing; repeat them at the top and the bottom of every messageD6 Prompt Engineering (placement, XML tags)Lesson 4, section 6.4

10After the switch, a few answers use a different capitalization of a category, or come back with stop_reason: "refusal"Check stop_reason before parsing, normalize and validate the value in code, and send anything that fails to a fallback path or a personTrust anything that matches the schema; drop failures silently; default every failure to "Other"D6 Output HandlingLesson 4, section 6.5

Scoring: 8 or more is a pass. Decisions 1 and 2 are the heart of the scenario: measure before you switch (an eval set and a baseline), and pull the cheap levers first: effort, prompt structure, caching and shorter output, then a smaller model only if the eval holds.
