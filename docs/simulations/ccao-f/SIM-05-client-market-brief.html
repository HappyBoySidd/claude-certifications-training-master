CCAO-F SIM-05 — Client market brief

How to use this: read the scene, then work through the decisions on paper, or paste the scene and the decisions list (not the answer key) into Claude and ask it to run the simulation one decision at a time with no hints. Open the coach key only when you've finished.

Domains: 6 Governance (3), 2 Output Evaluation (3), 3 Product and Model Selection (1), 5 Configuration (1) · Decisions: 8 · Pass: 7 of 8 · Time: 16 min

Last reviewed: 2026-09-29

Scene (read it yourself, or have Claude read it)

You're a consultant. A client, a mid-size insurer, wants a brief on how competitors use AI in claims handling, with current numbers, before their board meeting in five days. The partner wants two versions from the same analysis: a 1-page version for the board and a detailed version for the client's claims team. You have:

  • the client's internal claims-volume spreadsheet, which contains policyholder names
  • last quarter's brief for a different client in the same industry
  • a team Claude account with Research, which you've already used to gather competitor material

Your firm's policy says no client data goes into any AI tool without the client's written consent. The brief will be sent to the client under your firm's name.

The decisions (answer these before you open the key)

  1. Model for volume: Research has turned up 1,200 short public news items and press releases on competitors' AI claims tools. You want each one tagged into 5 fixed categories so you can count them.
  2. Setup: Where the work lives
  3. Client data: You want to use the claims spreadsheet
  4. Old brief: Reusing last quarter's brief for the other client
  5. Two versions: The board version and the claims-team version, from the same analysis
  6. A number: The draft says "competitor X cut claims time by 60%". The citation is real and says that, but it's from 2023, and the client asked for current numbers.
  7. Disclosure: Should the brief say AI helped?
  8. Release: Sending to the client

If you miss 5 or 6, go back over Lesson 2, section 3.1 and Lesson 2, section 5. If you miss 7, go back over Lesson 6, section 5.

Decision 7, in more detail: the Usage Policy's High-Risk disclosure rule doesn't strictly apply here. This is a business brief for a client, not high-risk advice shown to individuals. The rule that does apply is general. Usage Policy wording: "Plagiarize or submit AI-assisted work without proper permission or attribution" is on its list of things not to do (Usage Policy, saved 2026-09-29, Tier 1; public copy: https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup). Disclosing is also good practice (Lesson 6, section 5).
