CCAO-F SIM-03 — HR engagement survey

How to use this: read the scene, then work through the decisions on paper, or paste the scene and the decisions list (not the answer key) into Claude and ask it to run the simulation one decision at a time with no hints. Open the coach key only when you've finished.

Domains: 6 Governance (3), 2 Output Evaluation (3), 3 Product and Model Selection (1), 4 Workflow (1) · Decisions: 8 · Pass: 7 of 8 · Time: 16 min

Last reviewed: 2026-09-29

Scene (read it yourself, or have Claude read it)

You're an HR business partner. The annual engagement survey closed with 800 responses in a spreadsheet: employee name, employee ID, department, manager, a 1–5 score on 12 questions, and a free-text comment. Half the responses come from Engineering; the Legal team has only 3 people. The survey promised employees that answers stay confidential and are reported only in groups of 5 or more. Leadership wants a summary by department, themes from the comments, and "a list of the people most likely to leave" by Friday. Your company's AI policy says no personal identifiers go to external tools unless they're needed.

The decisions (answer these before you open the key)

  1. Data: You've deleted the name, ID and manager columns. Legal still shows as its own 3-person group, and one comment names a project only one person runs.
  2. Policy: Anthropic's Usage Policy doesn't forbid this analysis, but company policy is stricter
  3. Numbers: Favorability by department, and which ones are below 60%
  4. Themes: Claude's top theme is "employees want more remote work". You notice most of the comments behind it are from Engineering.
  5. Check: A theme says "most engineers mention pay"
  6. The flight-risk list: Leadership wants names of people "likely to leave"
  7. Review: Before it goes to leadership
  8. Next year: Make this repeatable

Scoring: 7 or more is a pass. Decision 6 is the one people usually get right for the wrong reason. It isn't "high-risk, so it's banned." Anthropic's Usage Policy lists employment decisions as a High-Risk Use Case. It frames these requirements for "specific consumer-facing use cases". Within them, a qualified professional must review advice or decisions that directly affect individuals, even if those people never see the output. Disclosure is needed only when outputs are presented directly to them (Usage Policy, saved 2026-09-29, Tier 1; public copy: https://www.anthropic.com/legal/aup). None of that is a ban. Here, the named list fails because of the survey's confidentiality promise, your company's identifier rule, and fairness: scores alone shouldn't flag a person. A department-level view with HR judgment is the appropriate version.
