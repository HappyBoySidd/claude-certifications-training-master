CCAR-P SIM-06 — Month-End Variance Commentary

How to use this: read the scene, then work through the decisions on paper, or paste the scene and the decisions list (not the answer key) into Claude and ask it to run the simulation one decision at a time with no hints. Open the coach key only when you've finished.

Primary domains: 2 Claude Models, Prompting & Context Engineering, 4 Evaluation, Testing & Optimization · Decisions: 10 · Time: 20 min

Scene (original, written from the official objectives)

An accounting firm closes the books for 3,000 client accounts every month. For each account an analyst writes a short commentary explaining why this month's numbers moved against last month's. The firm wants Claude to draft those commentaries from the account's ledger (often 100–180k tokens), a 20,000-token internal accounting policy, and a few lookup tools. Partners care about two things: every number and every cause in the commentary must be traceable to the ledger, and the monthly bill must stay predictable.

The decisions (answer these before you open the key)

  1. Which model to start with
  2. Drafts are good, but thinking tokens make the bill too high
  3. Commentaries drift in tone and structure between accounts
  4. The prompt puts the question first, then the ledger, then the policy; answers on big ledgers get worse
  5. The same policy and tool definitions go out on every call, and a timestamp sits at the very top
  6. The monthly run happens overnight and nobody waits on a single draft
  7. Deciding what "good" means before tuning
  8. A model-graded rubric scores "causes are supported"; nobody knows if the grader is right
  9. A draft blames a variance on a "one-off supplier refund" that isn't in the ledger
  10. A new model version is released and the team wants to switch next month
Answer key (coach key): don't open until you've finished the simulation
