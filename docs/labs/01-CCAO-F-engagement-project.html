Lab 01 — A Claude Project that does one job well (CCAO-F)

Last reviewed: 2026-09-29

Supports: CCAO-F Lesson 1: Prompting and Task Execution, Lesson 2: Output Evaluation and Validation, Lesson 5: Configuration and Knowledge Management, Lesson 6: Governance, Risk, and Responsible Use · Time: 60–90 minutes · Needs: a Claude account with Projects (claude.ai). No code.

Coming from a lesson? Lesson 5's "Try it" is steps 1–4 (build the Project). Lesson 2's "Try it" is steps 5–7 (grade 5 outputs). Doing the whole lab in one sitting is fine too.

Done when: a Project with written instructions and two knowledge files has produced 5 outputs, you've graded each one pass, revise or fail against a checklist you wrote first (including a bias check), at least one Revise or Fail has been fixed by changing one thing, and you've written down what changed.

Steps

  1. Pick one repeat task you do at work, with no customer data in it. For example: "turn my meeting notes into a status update for my manager".
  2. Create a Project. Write the instructions in four short parts:
    • who Claude is helping, and who reads the result
    • the task, in one sentence
    • the output format: headings, length, tone
    • what to do when information is missing (ask, or mark it [missing], but never invent it)
  3. Add knowledge. Upload two files: one good past example, and a one-page style guide. Name them with a version, such as style-guide-v1.md. When you change one, upload v2 and delete v1, so old guidance doesn't linger.
  4. Write the review checklist before you run anything. Keep it to 5 or 6 lines, for example: facts match the notes · no invented dates or names · under 200 words · the ask is in the first line · the tone suits the reader · fair and balanced: no group's view is missing or stereotyped, and it doesn't sound certain where things are contested.
  5. Run it on 5 inputs: two normal ones, one with missing information, one that's messy, and one that mentions several teams or people (to test the bias line).
  6. Grade all 5 outputs. For each, note whether it's complete, accurate and fit for the reader, then mark it Pass, Revise or Fail against your checklist. For every Revise or Fail, change one thing: the instructions, an example or the knowledge. Then rerun that input.
  7. Write the lesson learned in 3 lines in your own notes: what you changed, and what it fixed.

What this teaches

  • Clear instructions, context and examples beat "be careful" (Lesson 1).
  • You judge output against criteria you wrote first, not against a gut feel, and that includes bias (Lesson 2).
  • Knowledge needs versions and cleanup (Lesson 5).
  • Nothing sensitive goes in (Lesson 6).
