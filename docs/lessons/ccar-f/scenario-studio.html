Use one complete learning loop

Plain ↔ technical · stop_reason / tool_use

Related objective example. Keep the current case's facts and chosen answer separate. For a decision, choose before opening this study help.

In everyday language

Claude asks to look up an order. Your program performs the lookup, sends its observed result back, and asks Claude to continue. Stopping the model is separate from proving a payment finished.

Does not establish: tool_use is a request for client execution, not execution itself; end_turn is not business settlement.

In technical terms

stop_reason tool_use tool_result tool_use_id end_turn

Inspect the technical example

Actual Messages content-block shapes; fictional IDs/data; response and next-request excerpts, not a complete API request

Observed assistant response excerpt (stop_reason is RESPONSE metadata):
{"role":"assistant","stop_reason":"tool_use","content":[{"type":"tool_use","id":"toolu_order17","name":"lookup_order","input":{"order_id":"O4"}}]}

YOUR runtime executes the permitted lookup. For the next Messages request,
append only assistant role + content, followed by the matching result:
{"role":"assistant","content":[{"type":"tool_use","id":"toolu_order17","name":"lookup_order","input":{"order_id":"O4"}}]}
{"role":"user","content":[{"type":"tool_result","tool_use_id":"toolu_order17","content":"{\"order_id\":\"O4\",\"status\":\"delivered\"}"}]}

Send the updated messages array in the next model request.
Do not copy response-only stop_reason into an input message.

Read it as: The tool_use id and tool_result tool_use_id must match. Input messages contain role and content; stop_reason describes the response. The returned content is observed lookup evidence, not a credit receipt.

Official mechanism reference

  1. Understand: open a scenario below; name its goal, evidence, constraints and possible side effects.
  2. Decide before reading: write the mechanism you would choose and why the nearest alternative loses.
  3. Inspect: open the linked objective packet and its inline drawing, example code, prompt or contract. Explain the failure and recovery path aloud.
  4. Change one condition: reverse a deadline, authorization, evidence or dependency assumption. Explain whether your architecture changes.
  5. Test: answer the matching original question without the lesson open. Use its exact repair link after a miss; return to the same saved question.
  6. Recall: use the closed-answer cards and conversation scripts; attempt the different delayed retry when due.

Memory cue: GATES — Goal, Authority, Tools, Evidence, Stop/recover. This is an original recall aid, not an official exam framework. Always justify the actual mechanism; a mnemonic cannot decide the answer for you.

Transfer the decision across industries

Finance, Legal and Marketing receive dedicated practice below because they matter to this learner. Education and Government extend the transfer. This is a curriculum choice; no official industry weighting is claimed. All inputs and policies are fictional. Use the same Claude mechanisms while changing the business constraints.

Finance — approved payment changes after review

Situation: A treasury assistant proposes a payment of 4,000 units, obtains approval, then receives a corrected invoice for 4,600. The bank request later times out. Decide first: May it reuse approval? May it retry the payment?

Reveal the worked choice after deciding

Worked choice: Bind approval to the exact target, amount and proposal version. The changed amount requires a new valid approval. An uncertain write requires status reconciliation or a supported idempotency mechanism before another effect; end_turn does not prove settlement. A prompt saying “never pay twice” cannot enforce this.

Changed constraint: If the system only drafts advice and has no payment capability, a simpler advisory workflow may suffice. Produce: an approval-state table and unknown-result branch. Approval packet · Unknown effects · Flow D01

Inspect D01 here

Identify target and effect. Verify identity binding. Check current scoped approval. Block failed checks. Execute only the authorized effect. Record a receipt; unknown outcome requires status reconciliation before retry.

Legal — conflicting contract evidence and a missing specialist

Situation: Compare three fictional supplier contracts for retention, exit terms and integration cost. One specialist returns cost; retention evidence is absent and another source contradicts the draft conclusion. Decide first: What can be concluded now, and what must be investigated?

Reveal the worked choice after deciding

Worked choice: Use explicit shared criteria, source-tagged findings and a coordinator coverage ledger. Preserve useful partial findings, reopen the missing dimension and retain conflicting evidence. Do not call the comparison complete or let a critic certify a conclusion without independent source access.

Changed constraint: A single authorized clause lookup may need only one scoped agent. Produce: a claim/source/coverage table and a targeted recovery brief. Coordination packet · Independent critique · Flow D03

Inspect D03 here

Send an explicit brief to the isolated specialist. Require source-tagged findings and structured failures. Check mandatory dimensions. Preserve successes while recovering gaps or stopping dependent claims.

Marketing — campaign audience, ambiguous fields and a hard launch

Situation: A campaign assistant extracts audience attributes from inconsistent briefs. A record has no consent status; a local date is ambiguous; launch is in two hours. Decide first: Should it guess fields, send everything to batch, or hold dependent actions?

Reveal the worked choice after deciding

Worked choice: Model unknown/missing states explicitly, retain original text and source locators, validate meaning as well as shape, and keep the fictional application's consent prerequisite in executable authorization logic. Batch is appropriate only where its completion characteristics and independence fit the deadline. Do not silently convert missing consent into permission or assume a deadline guarantees completion.

Changed constraint: With independent overnight analysis and no launch dependency, batch may become reasonable. Produce: three validation outcomes, an audience-exclusion rule and a deadline fallback. Shape versus truth · Batch choice · Flow D04

Inspect D04 here

Separate protocol outcome from application payload. Empty successful records mean no match. Repair invalid arguments. Retry only eligible transient operations within a budget. Authorization needs changed permission. Uncertain writes need status lookup.

Education — tutoring history after compaction

Situation: A tutor resumes after its long conversation was summarized. The learner's accessibility requirement and unresolved misconception disappeared. Decide first: Is a longer context window sufficient?

Reveal the worked choice after deciding

Worked choice: Preserve explicit learning goals, constraints, misconceptions, source references and next checks in structured state, then verify that the summary retains them. A larger window cannot recreate omitted facts. Changed constraint: For a short session with all relevant facts already present, no elaborate memory workflow is necessary. Produce: a before/after context checklist. Context packet · Conversation practice.

Government — policy evidence changes during a paused case

Situation: An advisory service resumes a fictional permit case after permissions and a policy source changed. A second system returns a conflicting record. Decide first: Can it trust saved authorization and silently merge records?

Reveal the worked choice after deciding

Worked choice: Revalidate current authority before effects, retain source/time/identifier lineage through the join and surface unresolved conflicts. An MCP connection alone does not establish that the needed capability is included or authorized. Changed constraint: A public read-only lookup reduces the action boundary but still needs attributable evidence. Produce: a resume gate and source-preserving join. Resume packet · Source join · Connection versus inclusion.
