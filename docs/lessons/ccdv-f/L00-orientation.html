CCDV-F Lesson 0 — Orientation: how the Developer exam thinks

Time: 45 min · Do first: the baseline mock (step 3)

What you're preparing for

  • 53 items in 120 minutes. That's a little over 2 minutes per item.
  • Two item types: multiple-choice (pick one) and multiple-response (pick several). Each item tells you how many to pick. Count your ticks before moving on.
  • Pass mark: a scaled 720 on a 100–1,000 scale. It's a fixed standard, not a curve: you're measured against the bar, not against other people.
  • Your score report shows pass/fail, the scaled score, and percent correct per domain. The domain numbers don't decide the result, but they tell you what to fix if you retake.
  • Fee $125 USD (your partner tier may lower it at checkout).
  • Valid 12 months. Renewing on time is a free, unproctored assessment on the Anthropic Partner Academy. Let it lapse and you pay for the full exam again.
  • Retakes wait 14, then 30, then 90 days; at most 4 attempts in a rolling 12 months.

Item counts are 53 × weight, rounded; the guide gives only percentages.

The six rules that decide most questions

  1. Change the structure, not a knob. Wrong answers usually tweak a parameter: raise max_tokens, change temperature, "use a bigger model". Right answers change the design: a different API mode, a schema, a hook, a subagent.
  2. Ask "who is waiting?" before "what's cheapest?" A person waiting means the real-time Messages API (stream it). Nobody waiting and lots of volume means the Message Batches API. Same long prefix every call means prompt caching.
  3. Enforce in code what must always hold. A prompt line is a request; a hook, a schema or a missing tool is a guarantee. Untrusted content never gets to trigger sensitive actions.
  4. Pick the right home for a capability. One app: a custom tool. Many apps, maintained separately: an MCP server. A reusable procedure or convention: a Skill. File, shell and search basics: built-in tools.
  5. The cheapest structure that works wins. Known steps: a workflow, not an agent. Smallest model that passes your evals, not the biggest one. Tune effort before switching models.
  6. Pin it, version it, test the change. Pin model versions, version prompts and config, and treat a model upgrade as a change that needs an eval run, not a drop-in.

Rules 2–4 come straight from the rationales of the guide's three sample items (batch vs real time, prompt injection, MCP for reuse). Rules 1, 5 and 6 are our synthesis of the blueprint objectives.

What people who passed say

  • More Claude Code than the 3.1% weight suggests. CLI, CLAUDE.md, Skills, settings.json and subagents all came up. Confirmed: two colleagues who passed (INSIGHTS, 2026-09-26). Don't skip Lesson 7: Claude Code, Eval and Debugging; Claude Code also shows up inside Domain 2 (Configuration Management, Claude Application Design).
  • About two-thirds overlap with CCAR-F, so take it soon after. Single report: one colleague who passed.
  • Agent SDK, streaming and protocols, all inside scenario questions. Single report: one colleague who passed.
  • Harder than CCAR-P, with many development-specific questions. Single report: one colleague who passed.
  • One colleague reported 135 minutes and a 772 pass mark. The official guide says 120 minutes and a scaled 720; the guide wins. (The community cheat sheet also mentions "135 minutes of seat time", which probably includes the agreement and tutorial screens. Unconfirmed.)
  • Across all exams: full, timed mocks matter more than reading (Confirmed, four people), and some items ask for 2 or more answers (Confirmed, and in the guide).

Steps

Self-check

  • How many items, how long, what pass mark? (53, 120 minutes, scaled 720)
  • How do you know how many options to pick? (The item says so)
  • Which three domains make up about two-thirds? (Applications and Integration, Model Selection, Agents and Workflows)
  • Nobody is waiting and there are 20,000 requests. Which API? (Message Batches API)
  • Why does "add a line to the system prompt" lose to "add a hook"? (A prompt can be ignored; a hook runs every time)
