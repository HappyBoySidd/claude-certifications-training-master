CCAR-P Lesson 0 — Orientation: how the Professional exam thinks

Time: 45 min · Do first: the baseline mock (step 3)

What you're preparing for

  • 63 questions in 120 minutes, a little under 2 minutes each. The pass mark is a scaled 720 on a range of 100–1,000. The fee is $175 USD, before any partner discount shown at checkout.
  • Two item types: multiple-choice (pick one) and multiple-response (pick several). Every item tells you how many to pick. Count before you click.
  • Your score report shows pass or fail, the scaled score, and your percent correct per domain. Only the total decides the result.
  • No mandatory prerequisite. CCAR-F is recommended, not required. Anthropic said this to the Salesforce accelerated cohort (relayed by a colleague, September 2026): CCAR-F and CCAR-P "can be pursued independently; neither is a prerequisite for the other". Another colleague got the same answer earlier by email from Anthropic.
  • The credential lasts 12 months. Renewing on time is a free, non-proctored assessment on the Anthropic Partner Academy. If it lapses, you retake the full exam at full price.
  • Retakes wait 14, then 30, then 90 days, with at most 4 attempts in a rolling 12 months.

How this exam differs from CCAR-F

CCAR-F asks "how do you wire this up?" (agent loops, hooks, tool_choice, CLAUDE.md). CCAR-P asks "which design would you defend in a review, and why?" Most options are reasonable. Only one fits the constraint the scenario states. The scenarios come from industries such as finance, healthcare, retail and government.

The rules that decide most Professional questions

  1. Remove privilege, don't monitor it. If a role doesn't need a tool or permission, take it away. Logging, confirmation prompts and audits are compensating controls. They come second.
  2. Fix retrieval before blaming the model. Wrong answers straight after a data change point to chunking, embeddings or the index. They don't point to the model weights or the temperature.
  3. Measure before optimizing. Set the metrics and build the eval set first. Then change the prompt, the model or the architecture, and compare against that baseline.
  4. Use the least complex pattern that meets the requirement. A single call, then an augmented LLM, then a workflow, then an agent, then several agents. Each step up has to earn its place.
  5. Name the trade-off. Every choice costs something: accuracy, latency, money, safety or the effort to maintain it. The best answer says what you give up and why that's acceptable here.
  6. Put static content first and cache it. It's the one change that cuts both latency and cost.

Steps

Self-check

  • Which domain is the biggest? (Integration, 19%, about 12 questions)
  • Why does "log every refund" lose to "remove the refund tool" when staff never issue refunds? (Logging only detects misuse. Removing the tool means there's nothing left to misuse.)
  • A RAG bot starts giving wrong answers after a re-index, and nothing else changed. Where do you look first? (Retrieval and the index)
  • What must you check on every multiple-response item? (How many answers it asks for)

What people who passed say

  • It isn't harder than CCAR-F, and for some it was the easiest of the four. Confirmed by three colleagues who passed (one scored 944).
  • About half is standard system design with Claude wording (one colleague who passed, single report). Your general architecture experience counts. Spend your study time on the Claude-specific parts.
  • You don't need CCAR-F first (confirmed). Anthropic's two-day Partner Base Camp for architects does strongly recommend it first (single report, relayed by a colleague).
  • Across all exams: full, timed mocks beat reading, and some items ask for 2 or more answers (both confirmed). See Insights.
